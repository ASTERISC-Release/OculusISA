RULE 1: If the destination register for c.ldsp is x0, the instruction is reserved.
RULE 2: If c.sdsp uses x0 as the source register, the entire 64-bit operand is zero, and register x1 is not accessed.
RULE 3: c.ldsp computes its effective address by adding the zero-extended offset, scaled by 8, to the stack pointer, x2.
RULE 4: c.ld computes an effective address by adding the zero-extended offset, scaled by 8, to the base address in register rs1'.
RULE 5: c.sd computes an effective address by adding the zero-extended offset, scaled by 8, to the base address in register rs1'.
RULE 6: c.sdsp computes an effective address by adding the zero-extended offset, scaled by 8, to the stack pointer, x2.
RULE 7: An LD or SD instruction whose effective address is a multiple of 4 gives rise to two 4-byte memory operations.
RULE 8: Decomposed sequences resulting from a 4-byte-aligned LD or SD instruction are interruptible.
RULE 9: Exceptions may occur on subsequent operations resulting from decomposed LD or SD sequences, making the effects of previous operations within the same instruction visible.
